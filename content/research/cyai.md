## Abstract

Our research paper is motivated by organizational and economic risks from recent AI-enabled cybersecurity incidents and the critical need for “bilingual” professionals fluent in both cybersecurity and AI professional competencies to navigate the high-powered frontier of CyAI. This dual motivation framed our research question: *What cybersecurity and artificial intelligence (CyAI) converged competencies are expected of today’s computing professionals?* We define professional competencies as the knowledge, skills, and dispositions expected to perform one’s job role.

Our qualitative study used grey literature, i.e., timely evidence not currently published in academic literature, such as government workforce frameworks, industry reports, and white papers, using a combination of scoping and integrative review methods. The rigor of our study was ensured by purposive information source selection that considered 1) our professional experience and expertise from different domains, 2) our thorough documentation and verification of the data collection and analysis steps, and 3) the transparency and auditability of the entire review process.

In the final analysis, 272 professional competencies were identified. These competencies were categorized into 67 cyber-independent, 105 AI-independent, 61 cyber applied to AI, 31 AI applied to cyber, and 8 CyAI convergent. Competencies related to applying cyber to AI or applying AI to cyber were grouped into 15 𝐶𝑌 → 𝐴𝐼 cluster competencies and 5 𝐴𝐼 → 𝐶𝑌 cluster competencies. The iterative process of researcher synthesis and verification of the cluster competencies yielded the CyAI convergence. Our main contributions are the synthesis of professional cybersecurity and AI competencies into directionally coupled clusters and CyAI convergence competencies.
